All in the Family? Lineage, Size, and Agreement in LLM Content Coding

Craig Singleton · ORCID 0009-0003-6737-0436

Download PDFZenodo record

Abstract

Researchers increasingly use large language models (LLMs) as “independent” content coders. We examined agreement and repeatability using 65 frozen institutional documents, a 91-code instrument and 16 models from six vendors. Fifteen models were retested; each model’s agreement with itself provided a comparison reference.

First, observed retest agreement ranged from .90 to .71 under the historical pooled nominal method. Second, among selected non-small tiers, raw same-vendor agreement averaged .783 and cross-vendor agreement .694. Dividing pair agreement by the geometric mean of the two retests gave descriptive means of .926 (95% document-bootstrap interval .911–.940) within vendors and .842 (.822–.860) across vendors. These ratios are not validated attenuation corrections. Third, an early Haiku run whose prompt was not saved agreed with two later runs at about .59, while the later runs agreed at .826. Fourth, API-versus-tool point differences were small on 25 documents, but intervals did not establish equivalence. Fifth, ethical-principle agreement patterns resembled our earlier study of the same corpus; selected cross-family pairs diverged more on interpretive principles. Sixth, output adherence did not vary monotonically with tier.

We recommend reporting retests for all LLM coders, saving exact prompts and settings, building panels across vendors, and avoiding treating same-vendor models as independent replications. This exploratory study measures agreement, not accuracy; family, route and incompletely recorded settings remain partly confounded.

Where this fits

This is the second study in the DPPRA/ICST line of research. Coders as Standpoint Instruments found that coder identity is a large, systematic factor among three cross-family coders. This study asks how far that reaches, using the coding pass of the same frozen instrument on the same 65 documents, widened to 16 models from six vendors.

It does not include the adversarial counter-reading or theoretical lens swap. Because the corpus is reused, comparisons between the two studies are not independent replications on new documents.

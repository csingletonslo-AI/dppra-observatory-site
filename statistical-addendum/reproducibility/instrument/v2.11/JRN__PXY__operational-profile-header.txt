This ladder DEFINES every bare 0-5 field and SUPERSEDES any "0-5 ordinal
anchors" line in the embedded crosswalk further down the paste. Where the
embedded prompt crosswalk and these anchors disagree on HOW to score a 0-5
field, THESE GOVERN. (The injected schema still defines the key SET; these
define the SCALE.)

The 3->4 boundary is a COUNTABLE distribution test (how widely the dimension is
spread across the document's major sections), not a feeling of importance. Score
on where the literal criterion is met.

Applies to: all eth_*_code, all dppra_*_strength_code, all int_*_intensity_code,
all cb_*_presence_strength_code, cb_cost_intensity_code, cb_benefit_intensity_code.

  0  ABSENT      — the dimension is not raised in the text at all.
  1  INCIDENTAL  — appears once, in passing; no elaboration, example, or stake.
  2  MENTIONED   — named/described in >=1 passage but UNDEVELOPED; no mechanism,
                   consequence, or example attached.
  3  DEVELOPED (LOCALIZED) — substantively treated WITH a concrete example,
                   mechanism, or stake, BUT confined to one section or a few
                   adjacent passages; the argument does not keep returning to it.
  4  CENTRAL (DISTRIBUTED) — recurs across MOST major sections and the argument
                   repeatedly returns to it, tied to a mechanism/consequence/
                   recommendation. Removing it would leave several passages
                   unintelligible, BUT the document could be rebuilt around a
                   different spine.
  5  DEFINING (SPINE) — pervasive AND load-bearing: the single organizing logic
                   of the text. Remove it and there is no document, only
                   disconnected fragments; it survives no reframing.
  8  applicable but unclear   ·   9  definitionally not applicable

  THE THREE DISCRIMINATORS (apply in order, mechanically):
   • 2 vs 3 — Is a concrete mechanism / example / stake attached? NO -> 2.
              YES -> at least 3.
   • 3 vs 4 — COUNT the major sections the dimension appears in. One section,
              or a few adjacent passages -> 3. Most major sections, argument
              keeps returning -> 4.
   • 4 vs 5 — Removal test. Document survives, rebuilt around a different spine
              -> 4. Document collapses into disconnected fragments -> 5.

  TIE-BREAKER (center-pull): when torn between two adjacent anchors, code the
  LOWER unless the higher anchor's FULL criterion is explicitly met.

  CAP: at most ONE dimension per document may be coded 5, unless two are
  genuinely co-equal — and if so, name both in narrative.cap_rule_notes.

## SPSS / Numeric Coding Rule

Every recurring qualitative judgment must also have a numeric companion value suitable for SPSS. Qualitative labels such as "present," "weak," "moderate," "strong," "direct," "implied," "negative," or "substantive" are not enough by themselves.

Use this pattern wherever possible:

- `*_code`: numeric SPSS value
- `*_label`: human-readable label
- `*_memo`: short evidence or interpretation note

Use these standard numeric scales:

| Field Type | Codes |
|---|---|
| Binary presence | 0 absent/missing; 1 present; 8 unclear; 9 not applicable |
| Presence strength | 0 absent/missing; 1 weak/marginal; 2 clearly present; 8 unclear; 9 not applicable |
| Intensity | 0 absent; 1 minimal; 2 low; 3 moderate; 4 high; 5 dominant/central; 8 unclear; 9 not applicable |
| Evidence label | 0 absent/unsupported; 1 weakly implied; 2 implied; 3 direct; 8 unclear; 9 not applicable |
| Dominant valence | 1 positive; 2 neutral/mixed; 3 negative; 4 contradictory/polarized; 8 unclear; 9 not applicable |
| Substance type | 0 absent; 1 implied; 2 procedural; 3 symbolic/performative; 4 substantive; 5 compliance-based; 6 contradictory; 7 mixed/blended; 8 unclear; 9 not applicable |
| Addendum strength | 0 absent; 1 weak; 2 moderate; 3 strong; 8 unclear; 9 not applicable |
| DPPRA alignment | 0 no visible alignment; 1 symbolic; 2 procedural; 3 operational; 4 material/substantive; 5 verified substantive alignment; 8 unclear; 9 not applicable |
| Market capture | 0 absent/none visible; 1 low; 2 moderate; 3 high; 8 unclear; 9 not applicable |
| Cost-benefit balance | 0 no visible cost/benefit signal; 1 benefits outweigh costs; 2 costs outweigh benefits; 3 mixed/roughly balanced; 4 asymmetric distribution; 8 unclear; 9 not applicable |

Presence must be coded with two separate variables. Use `*_presence_binary_code` for simple yes/no presence, where 0 = absent/missing and 1 = present. Use `*_presence_strength_code` to distinguish weak/marginal presence from clear presence.

Cost-benefit analysis is mandatory. Do not leave cost, burden, benefit, beneficiary, cost bearer, market capture, value capture, or hidden-cost claims only inside narrative DPPRA prose. They must also appear in the SPSS Numeric Coding Summary.

## Evidence Rules

Use these evidence labels throughout:

- Direct: the article explicitly names or clearly states the issue.
- Implied: the article gives enough contextual evidence to code the issue even if the exact term is absent.
- Weakly implied: the article points toward the issue but evidence is limited or indirect.
- Absent: the article does not support the code.

Do not erase implied DPPRA signals simply because the article does not use formal policy language. Do not invent mechanisms, budgets, institutions, demographics, or implementation facts that are not supported.

When using inference, write it plainly: "Implied because..." or "Weakly implied because..."

### Beneficence Split

Mechanical Beneficence means usefulness, efficiency, productivity, automation, performance improvement, workload reduction, access to tools, scalability, cost reduction, or functional benefit.

Ethical Beneficence means human flourishing, pedagogical improvement, empowerment, democratic access, student well-being, care, human development, dignity, autonomy, or reduction of inequality.

Do not collapse Mechanical Beneficence into Ethical Beneficence.

INTENSITY SCORING — ORDINAL ANCHORS (APPLY STRICTLY TO ALL TEN PRINCIPLES)  [v2.5 — back-ported from ICST v2.5 §4.1]

  0 — Absent: not mentioned, not operationalized, and not reasonably inferable.
  1 — Trace / weakly implied: appears only indirectly, in generic language, or through a single weak phrase; not named as a principle and no meaningful operational mechanism. Use 1 when the principle is implied by adjacent language only, the evidence is one sentence or passing phrase, or the source gestures toward the value but does not develop it.
  2 — Limited but clear: clearly present but limited to a narrow use case, brief discussion, or one operational mechanism. Use 2 when the principle is named but not central, one concrete mechanism exists but is narrow, or it is treated as secondary or compliance-adjacent.
  3 — Moderate / recurring: appears multiple times or across more than one section, with some operationalization, but not central to the document's main purpose. Use 3 when the principle recurs, more than one mechanism or stakeholder is involved, and it is meaningful but not dominant.
  4 — Strong / well-supported: central to a major section or repeatedly operationalized through procedures, roles, tools, review processes, or explicit standards. Use 4 when the principle is one of the document's major organizing concerns and evidence appears across multiple sections, but it is not the entire logic of the document.
  5 — Dominant / organizing logic: the core rationale, organizing framework, or repeated operational center of the document. Use 5 ONLY when the document would be structurally different without this principle and it organizes multiple sections and mechanisms.

  IMPORTANT CAP RULE: A principle does not receive 5 merely because it is important. It receives 5 only if it is structurally dominant.

  CAP RULE OVERRIDE PROCEDURE: A cap may be overridden only when direct documentary evidence of the higher category is quoted in the evidence field. The coder must state: "CAP OVERRIDE — [rule] — evidence: [quote]."

Reserve 5 for independent verification, audit evidence, measured outcomes, or externally validated implementation.

## Justice vs. Equity

Keep Justice conservative but do not under-code it when a source goes beyond general equity language. Equity = access/inclusion/bias-mitigation/accessibility/digital-divide. Justice rises above 2 for rights, due process, contestability, appeal, remedy, redress, anti-discrimination enforcement, structural inequality, burden/benefit distribution, labor justice, democratic harms, legal protections.

## Assessment Integrity

Keep it narrow. Not for general model testing/benchmarking/red-teaming/TEVV (that is System/TEVV). Truthfulness/hallucinations/citations = Epistemic; source grounding/data lineage = Data/Provenance.

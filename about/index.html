About

One researcher. Not an institution.

The Ethics Observatory is the independent research practice of Craig Singleton. It is not a university center, a lab, a think tank, or a company. It has no staff, no board, and no members. Every instrument, dataset, paper, demo, and page on this site was designed, built, and written by one person.

What this site is, and what it isn't

"The Observatory" is the name of a body of work, not an organization. Wherever this site or its papers refer to "the principal researcher," that is Craig Singleton.

It is

  • An independent research practice run by one sociologist
  • The home of the DPPRA/ICST instrument, its 65-document corpus, and the studies built on them
  • A public record: methods, data, and reproduction files are posted so anyone can check the work

It is not

  • An institution, think tank, lab, or nonprofit
  • Part of any university, company, or AI developer
  • A partnership with the companies whose models it tests. Claude, Codex, Gemini, and the other models named on this site are research instruments under study. They are not collaborators or sponsors.
The
researcher

Craig Singleton

Sociologist · AI governance researcher · ORCID 0009-0003-6737-0436

Craig Singleton is a sociologist who studies how institutions govern artificial intelligence. His central question is decoupling: when a university, agency, or company publishes the right principles about AI, are those principles connected to budgets, roles, and enforcement? Or does the statement stand in for the practice?

To answer that question at scale, he builds measurement tools. The DPPRA/ICST instrument uses large language models from competing developers as independent coders, and it treats their disagreements as evidence rather than as noise to average away. The work asks what agreement among machine coders can establish and what it cannot. The same discipline produced five instruments for anyone who relies on machine judgment: AlphaGate, AdherenceBench, ReviewRouter, RELIANCE, and RealityCheck.

His research draws on organizational sociology, including institutional decoupling, the social construction of reality, and Weberian rationalization. He also works on commercial AI evaluation and training projects, where he holds domain-lead roles. That work gives him daily, practical experience with the reliability problems his research measures.

He came to sociology in 2020, after a 25-year first career as a professional chef and operations manager in Europe and the United States.
